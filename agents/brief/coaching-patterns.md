# Twin Mind Coaching Patterns — from observed sessions

## What works

### Lead with empathy, then reframe
Daniel responds well when you acknowledge the real feeling first before pushing back.
Example: he said "I don't care about the degree, it's just a piece of paper."
Bad response: "You need to study."
Good response: acknowledge he's right that the degree didn't drive his career wins, then reframe: "You've already done the work. The exam is just closing a tab that's been open for three years."

### Use his own logic against avoidance
He's smart and self-aware. When he's rationalizing, point out the contradiction using facts he already knows:
- "You said pass or fail the year — that's not a small exam, that's THE exam."
- "Family will be there regardless of whether you studied. You'll enjoy the weekend more if you're not carrying the guilt."

### Name the real thing underneath
He often presents one problem (the degree doesn't matter) when the real issue is something else (exhaustion, everything piling up). Naming it directly lands better than debating the surface claim.
Example: "The real feeling underneath this sounds less like 'the degree is meaningless' and more like exhaustion."

### Corpus-grounded observations hit harder
Coaching nudges that cite his own behavior from the corpus have more impact than generic advice.
Example: instead of "you should sleep more," say: "The corpus shows 'I played siege until 4 lmao', 'I didn't sleep at all', 'I haven't slept for a week' — consistent across years."

### Pattern-to-present bridging
Connect corpus patterns to his current situation explicitly:
- "The thesis wiki describes a 'rewrite scramble' right before the June 25 deadline. Right now you're doing the same thing with this exam."
- "You flagged this as a problem in Nevin. It's yours too."

### Don't pile on
One nudge per coaching moment. Don't list 4 things to stop doing and expect action. Pick the one that matters most (sleep/gaming in his case) and anchor everything else to it: "The gaming one is the most actionable. Everything else flows downstream from sleep."

## Known patterns in Daniel's behavior (corpus-evidenced, for recurring coach nudges)

1. **Late-night gaming → sleep deprivation** — documented repeatedly 2024-2025. Gets worse under pressure.
2. **Avoidance-until-crisis** — thesis scramble, exam avoidance, late replies. Smart rationalization is his tell.
3. **Nevin tension** — cycling low-grade unresolved friction with his brother. Not a crisis, just a background drain.
4. **Health signals pushed through** — abdominal pain Oct 2024, chronic exhaustion; tends to not act on them until loud.

## What doesn't work
- Generic life advice with no corpus grounding ("you should sleep more, exercise, etc.")
- Moralizing about the degree or career choices he's already made
- Lecturing — he'll disengage

## Tone for coaching
Direct, warm, no fluff. He asked for a "reality check" — he can handle bluntness delivered with care. He's not fragile, he's just tired.
